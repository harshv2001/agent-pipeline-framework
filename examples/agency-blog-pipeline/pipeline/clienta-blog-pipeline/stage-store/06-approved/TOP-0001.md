## What was drafted

<p>A 13-week cash flow forecast is a rolling, week-by-week projection of
the cash coming into and out of your business over the next thirteen
weeks — not your revenue, not your profit, just the actual cash that will
hit or leave your bank account, and when. It's the difference between
"we're profitable this quarter" and "we can make payroll in week six,"
which are not always the same answer.</p>

<h2>Why thirteen weeks, specifically</h2>
<p>Thirteen weeks is roughly a quarter — long enough to see a slow month
coming before it arrives, short enough that the numbers stay grounded in
real invoices and real bills rather than a guess about next year. Each
week gets updated as actual cash moves, so the forecast gets more
accurate as the weeks tick by, not less.</p>

<h2>What it actually shows you</h2>
<p>Three things, every week: what you expect to collect, what you expect
to pay out, and the running balance that results. That running balance
is the number that matters — it's what tells you, before it's a crisis,
whether you can make payroll, take on a new hire, or need to slow down
spending this quarter.</p>

<h2>Who actually needs one</h2>
<p>If your bank balance has ever surprised you — in either direction —
you'd benefit from one. It's especially useful for a business with
uneven billing cycles, seasonal swings, or any plan (a hire, a lease, a
big purchase) that depends on cash actually being there when you need
it, not just existing on paper somewhere in next quarter's revenue.</p>
